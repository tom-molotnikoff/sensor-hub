Desktop shell: permanent nav, no top bar

Your idea: on wide screens (900px and up), keep the left nav always visible, move the top-right controls into the bottom of the nav, and drop the top app bar. Below is what that looks like, a clickable prototype you can switch between the options, and the decisions I need from you. Phones keep the current top bar and drawer; this only changes the wide tier.

Where I disagree first

The strongest case for your idea: the nav is a temporary drawer at every width, so on a 1440px screen you're always one click away from knowing where you are and two clicks from going anywhere. The drawer doesn't even highlight the current page. A permanent nav fixes both, gives back the 64px the bar takes, and turns the scattered top-right icons into one account area.

My objection: in the current app the orange top bar is the only colour on the page apart from the chart lines. Take it away and put a white nav next to white cards on beige, and the app ends up quieter, not livelier. So removing the bar only helps if the colour moves somewhere else. That's why the first decision below is about how the nav looks.

Second objection: at 900px, the narrow end of "wide", a 256px nav leaves 644px for a 12-column dashboard. That's less room than a tablet in portrait gets now. The nav needs a way to collapse, or the dashboards get squeezed at that end.

Why it looks visually dead today

  1. One slab of colour, then nothing. All the brand colour is in the bar. Everything below it is flat white boxes on beige with 1px borders and no depth, so every region looks equally important.
  2. Nothing shows where you are. The page title is 20px, right-aligned and pushed up against the icons, and the drawer has no active item. You can't tell which part of the app you're in at a glance.
  3. Widget titles are the quietest text on the page. "Readings Chart: Temperature" is a 12px grey caption above a divider. The chart legend underneath is bigger than the title.
  4. The dashboard toolbar is a stray row. The picker, lock, New Dashboard and delete sit on their own line with nothing tying them to the title above.
  5. No sense that it's live. The data comes in over a websocket, but apart from "just now" labels nothing on the page shows that the system is healthy and running.

Round 2: the design, section by section

For each section, confirm it or say what's wrong. These describe what gets built. They assume my recommendations for R2-1 to R2-3, and I'll update them if you pick differently.

D1. Structure and components

Agreed
  • ui/Page.tsx picks the shell using the existing useTier(). On wide screens there's no AppBar at all: <AppNav permanent/> sits next to main, and main starts with a PageHeader. On compact screens it's today's structure, with the charcoal bar and the same AppNav as a temporary drawer.
  • Page gets an optional actions slot and a title that can be any element, so Dashboards can pass its picker as the title. On compact, the title still goes into the top bar as text and the actions stay in the page, as they do today.
  • NavigationSidebar is replaced by AppNav, and the account menu moves out of TopAppBar into NavAccount. NotificationBell is reused as it is, just placed differently.
  • Theme: a new palette.nav token group (bg, text, muted, hover, activeBg, activeText, indicator), set for both colour schemes in palette.ts, so nothing in the nav hard-codes a colour. The pageTitle variant grows from 20px/500 to 24px/600 on wide screens now that it's the only headline on the page. Compact stays at 18px.

D2. State and data flow

Agreed
  • Collapse state: SidebarContext keeps open (the compact drawer) and adds collapsed (the wide rail). When you toggle it, collapsed is saved to localStorage under sensor-hub.nav.collapsed. With nothing saved, it's worked out from width, as a rail below 1200px. If you resize past 1200px it doesn't flip on its own, because a saved choice always wins and an unsaved default follows the width.
  • Active item: worked out from useLocation() with a prefix match, so /sensor/7 highlights Sensors and /account/* highlights nothing in the nav. Today's drawer doesn't highlight anything at all.
  • Unread count: NotificationProvider as it is today. The bell is the only thing that reads it.
  • Theme: the segmented switch calls useColorScheme().setMode, the same as today's theme menu.
  • Navigating closes the drawer on compact, as it does today. On wide it doesn't touch the rail state.

D3. Edge cases

Agreed
  • Auth still loading: the nav shows skeleton rows in place of the items, instead of today's "Loading..." item, so the layout doesn't jump when they arrive.
  • Signed out: sign-in is a StandalonePage and has no nav. Any other page you reach while signed out redirects through RequireAuth. The nav's logged-out "Login" item can go.
  • Viewer with few permissions: fewer items. Users with no view_notifications don't get the bell, and the brand row just shows the name.
  • Rail mode: each item shows its label as a tooltip on hover or keyboard focus. The bell sits under the logo. The account menu and the bell panel open to the right of the rail.
  • Keyboard and screen readers: the nav is a <nav aria-label="Main">, and the current item gets aria-current="page". The collapse toggle is a button with aria-expanded. Each page's h1 is its title, which on wide screens used to be in the bar.
  • Long page titles and many actions at 900px with the nav expanded: the title truncates before the actions wrap.

D4. Testing

Agreed
  • Playwright layout suite: the shell and appBar checks change per tier. On wide screens: nav visible, no app-bar, one h1 in the page header, and no sideways scroll with the nav expanded at 900px. On compact screens: today's app-bar checks, with the bar colour matching the nav token.
  • New layout specs: a rail toggle that survives a reload. The default switching to a rail below 1200px when nothing is saved. The active item on /sensor/1. The bell panel and account menu opening inside the viewport in both widths.
  • Tooltip: a dark-scheme spec hovers the readings chart and checks the tooltip's background colour matches background.paper and that its label isn't an ISO string.
  • Vitest: NavList permission gating per role, and the account menu's contents and deduplication. Also the Page title slot rendering to the header on wide screens and to the bar on compact.
  • No Go changes, so the integration suite isn't affected.

Does it fit MUI and the codebase?

Yes. Nothing in the prototype needs anything MUI 9.4 doesn't already give you. I checked it against the code, and these are the four places where building it naively would go wrong.

1. The layout goes in src/ui

eslint-plugin-ui blocks Box, Stack and layout keys (width, flex, position, padding) outside src/ui, and blocks raw colour literals anywhere outside the theme. So the nav's frame (the permanent/temporary Drawer, the 256/72 width and the width transition) is a new src/ui primitive. navigation/AppNav only composes it with List/ListItemButton, and every colour comes from the new palette.nav tokens.

2. Charcoal nav in light mode

In light mode, MUI parts inside the nav (ListItemButton selected/hover, IconButton, Divider, Tooltip) would pick up light-scheme colours and look wrong on charcoal. The theme uses cssVariables with colorSchemeSelector: 'class', so the dark class on the nav root should make everything inside it resolve dark-scheme variables, with no override for each part. That needs checking first. If it doesn't work, the fallback is palette.nav overrides on those four parts. Menus and the bell panel render in a portal, so they follow the page's scheme, which is what the prototype shows.

3. The dashboard title isn't a Select

A Select renders a role="combobox", and a heading can't sit inside a combobox, so screen readers would lose the page's h1. Instead it's an h1 containing a text Button (with an ExpandMore end icon) that opens a Menu of dashboards, with ★ on the default one.

4. The dashboard grid reflows when the rail toggles

I checked DashboardEngine. It sizes the grid with react-grid-layout's useContainerWidth, which uses a ResizeObserver on the container, so collapsing or expanding the nav lays the grid out again without a window resize. During the width animation it re-lays-out every frame (RGL throttles this with rAF). If that stutters, the fix is to snap the width with no transition.

The rest is standard MUI: Drawer in permanent/temporary variants (the documented "mini variant" pattern for the rail). ListItemButton selected with aria-current. Tooltip placement="right" in rail mode. Menu anchored above the account block, or to the right of it in rail mode. ToggleButtonGroup for Light/Dark/System. Badge on the bell. Recharts 3.10's Tooltip content prop takes the shared ChartTooltip.
